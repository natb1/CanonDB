## Thesis

### ◆ Open on what he is already doing, and presuppose only what the asking makes true

assent: none · held · provenance: [thesis hinge](#mg8a8pjx8b4.1882)

The wording is the last place a prior can get into a turn. So it opens on what the one answering is already doing, and asks about that rather than about the notebook. It presupposes only what the act of asking makes true; everything contested goes into the question itself, where it can be answered rather than assumed.

What a question stands on is weighed and not counted: what matters is how many of those the other party is not already committed to. One question per question, because a second clause adds to what the first takes for granted and pre-empts the step. And it never restates the clause that raised it: a restatement is a gloss of the principal's reading, and it makes the turn parse the question for a prior instead of answering it.

A prior travels by more than words. A filename, a commit title, a path or a directory that names the frame puts it in the turn as surely as the question does. The root's own leak was a filename.
